1

Identify

Before anyone talks price, figure out whether the airline considers this a group.

Owned by You

What qualifies as Group Air

Group Air is generally required when 10 or more passengers are traveling together on the same itinerary, or share at least one qualifying common flight.

That rule comes from the airline, not from Classic. Airline Group Departments use it to manage inventory and flight loads.

Same itinerary

LAX → CUN → LAX12 passengers traveling together. Group Air.

Different origins, one common flight

TUL → DFW → PUJ7 passengers
OKC → DFW → PUJ3 passengers
DFW → PUJAll 10 share this flight. May qualify.

Carrier requirements vary. Once the total group reaches 10 or more with a common itinerary or shared flight, submit it to Group Air for evaluation and let them confirm.

Count the entire traveling group

Don't count one Classic booking. Count everyone traveling together.

Booking 14 passengers
Booking 24 passengers
New request4 more passengers. The group is now 12.

The new request goes through Group Air, even though no single booking holds 10 people.

Don't split to avoid it. Dividing a larger group into several smaller FIT bookings works around the airline's rule, not ours. Keep it together and send it to Group Air.

Why Group Air is different from FIT air

Group Air is not FIT airfare for more people. It's a separate contract with its own clock. Depending on the carrier, it can include:

  • Contract acceptance deadline
  • Deposit amount and deposit deadline
  • Cancellation rules and a space-reduction allowance
  • Passenger-name deadline and ticketing/final-payment deadline
  • Name-change and deviation policies
  • Seating process and schedule-change protections

The real advantage. Many carriers allow a full passenger replacement within set limits. That's a name change, not a name correction, and it's generally not possible on standard FIT air.

What Group Air is not

  • Not a guaranteed discount. Group and FIT pricing can be similar. Compare FIT fares while researching if it helps, but never promise Group Air will be cheaper.
  • Usually no child fare. Most contracts price adults and children at the same group fare.
  • Not refundable. All Group Air tickets are considered non-refundable under current guidance. Cancellation penalties vary by carrier.

Say it like this. "Group Air isn't about a lower fare. It's about protection: held space, a contract, and in many cases the ability to swap a traveler if plans change. I'll get you the exact terms so you can decide."

Request

Group Air quotes what you send. Send a decision, not a search.

Owned by YouAdvisor

Research the flights first

Don't ask Group Air to "look for flights." Do the research, then let the advisor choose.

1. Research options in Plex or an approved flight-shopping resource

  • Sensible routing and the preferred carrier, if there is one
  • Reasonable departure and arrival times
  • The specific flight numbers
  • FIT pricing for comparison, when useful

2. Review the options with the advisor

Ask which itinerary they want quoted. Don't pass several undefined possibilities to Group Air when the advisor hasn't picked a schedule.

3

Proposal

A price is not a promise. Read what's actually being held before the advisor sees it.

Owned by YouGroup Air

What Group Air sends back

Depending on the carrier and request, the response can include:

  • Airline, flight schedule, and per-person group fare
  • Status of the requested space: held, or quote only
  • Contract acceptance deadline
  • Deposit amount and deposit deadline
  • Cancellation/reduction deadline
  • Name deadline and final-payment/ticketing deadline
  • Airline-specific restrictions
  • The TSA/passenger-name template, when applicable

Confirmed space vs. quote only

This is the distinction that protects the advisor's credibility with their client, and yours with the advisor.

Confirmed / space held

The airline is holding the group space through the stated deadline, subject to the terms Group Air sent. Think of it like space held in a PNR.

Quote only / no seats held

Pricing without space. Nothing is secured, the fare isn't guaranteed, and availability can change. Group Air must recheck price and space when the advisor decides. It may be higher.

Watch for language like QUOTE ONLY, NO SEATS BEING HELD, or SUBJECT TO AVAILABILITY.

Never assume. Most domestic carriers typically hold space and some international carriers quote only, but that's a pattern, not a rule. Read the actual Group Air email every time.

Say it like this (quote only). "This is pricing only. The airline isn't holding seats yet, so when you're ready I'll have Group Air recheck availability, and the fare could move. If this schedule matters to your client, the sooner the better."

Review the proposal before you send it

Verify the itinerary

  • Airline, flights, and dates are correct
  • Departure and arrival times match what was requested

Find every deadline

  • Contract acceptance
  • Deposit due date
  • Cancellation/reduction
  • Passenger names
  • Final payment/ticketing

Read the carrier-specific terms

  • Cancellation penalties and reduction allowance
  • Name-change and deviation rules
  • Seating restrictions and any special terms

Unclear? Ask Group Air before you interpret anything for the advisor.

Presenting Group Air to the advisor

Send the complete proposal, not just the fare. The advisor needs to see:

  • The flights offered and the per-person price
  • Whether space is actually held
  • When the group must accept
  • The deposit and its deadline
  • Reduction/cancellation terms
  • The name deadline and final-payment/ticketing deadline
  • Carrier rules that apply

Don't summarize away the penalties. The conditions that cost money are the ones the advisor most needs to see.

Several days went by? Confirm with Group Air that the pricing and space are still valid before you promise them.

4

Accept

Build it before you announce it, so Group Air can finish their part the moment your email lands.

Owned by YouGroup Air

The advisor says yes: what to do, in order

1. Prepare the Classic booking(s)

Create any additional bookings. When there's more than one, establish one Master Booking and cross-reference every related booking to it.

2. Build Customer Air in Plex

Do this before you email Group Air, so the booking is ready for them when your acceptance arrives.

3. Reply on the existing Group Air email

Tell Group Air the advisor is accepting, the Customer Air is entered, and list every applicable Classic booking number. Don't start a new email.

4. Add the required deposit fee to the booking

This is yours to add. Confirm timing with Group Air if you're unsure.

5. Start tracking the contract deadlines

From the dates in the Group Air terms, not from memory or another group.

Customer Air in Plex

Group Air lives in Plex as Customer Air (manual air). It's to flights what manually created, CAD-type inventory is to other components: entered by hand, not maintained as a live air booking.

That's why it won't update itself. Airline schedule changes don't flow into Customer Air. You update it manually (see Schedule changes).

You enter

Airline, flight number, departure and arrival points, date, departure and arrival times, adult/child quantities, and other required itinerary details. Match the Group Air itinerary exactly.

Group Air completes

Group Air pricing, supplier comments, customer comments, group name, airline group PNR, and number of seats held.

Hands off the Group Air comments. Don't overwrite or edit the supplier/customer comments Flights entered unless they ask you to.

The Classic Group Air contract

The official Classic Vacations Group Air contract normally arrives once the group is accepted and/or the deposit is secured. Group Air sends it to you.

Every airline's terms, cancellation rules, name-change rules, deviation rules, and penalties differ. Read it carefully before forwarding it to the advisor.

Advisor wants the contract before committing? Ask Group Air. They can provide it in advance on request.

5

Deposit & deadlines

Missed dates don't generate a warning. They release the seats.

Owned by YouAdvisor

The five milestones and who tracks them
  1. Contract acceptance
  2. Deposit
  3. Reduction / cancellation
  4. Passenger names
  5. Final payment / ticketing

Use the dates Group Air supplied for this group. Not industry averages, not a previous group, not the ranges in older training material.

You own the calendar. Group Air makes every effort to remind you, especially about deposits. Treat that as a backup, never as your tracking system.

Contract acceptance deadline

Some carriers allow only a few days to accept. Miss it, and the held space may cancel automatically.

If the advisor comes back later, Group Air has to return to the carrier, recheck availability, and possibly re-price. Don't promise the original fare or space.

Deposits and the deposit deadline

Deposit amounts and terms vary by carrier. A deposit may be refundable under specific conditions, non-refundable, applied to the final balance, or partially forfeited if space is reduced. Follow the exact terms Group Air provides.

Reminders come to you. Group Air will generally remind you when the deposit is due. The reminder doesn't go to the advisor, so passing it on is on you.

Missed the deposit deadline? The group space releases automatically. If the group still wants the air, contact Group Air for current availability and pricing.

Say it like this. "Your deposit is due on [date]. If it isn't in by then, the airline releases the seats and we'd be starting over at whatever the fare is that day."

Reducing or cancelling group space

Some contracts include a window to reduce unused space without penalty. After that deadline, the carrier may:

  • Keep some or all of the deposit
  • Charge a cancellation penalty
  • Limit the percentage of seats that can be reduced
  • Hold the group responsible for a minimum percentage of contracted space

Never promise penalty-free. Not until you've read the contract. If you're unsure, ask Group Air.

6

Names & seats

The name list is where small assumptions become airport problems.

Owned by YouAdvisorGroup Air

The passenger name list

At the right stage, Group Air sends the carrier's name template. Templates differ by airline.

Every name must match the travel document the traveler will use, exactly: first, middle, last, and any other requested details.

A blank middle name isn't an answer. If the list comes back without one, confirm with the advisor that the traveler truly has no middle name on their travel document. Then submit.

Say it like this. "I see no middle name for three travelers. Can you confirm their passports don't show one? It's much easier to fix today than at check-in."

Send the completed list on the existing Group Air thread.

Seating and name-list order

Group Air may use the order of the list to decide who sits together. They don't know the relationships in the group unless the list shows them.

Smith, JohnSmith, MarySmith, EmmaJones, Robert
Jones, Linda

Put travelers who want to sit together next to each other. Once seats are assigned, rearranging them can be difficult.

Seats aren't guaranteed. Airline operational or equipment changes can move them.

7

Changes

Name the change correctly and the carrier rule tells you the rest.

Owned by YouGroup Air

Name correction vs. name change
Name correction

Same traveler, name needs fixing.

Jon Smith → John Smith

Name change / replacement

Original traveler isn't going. Someone else takes the seat.

John Smith → Robert Jones

Many carriers permit replacement under set conditions. Rules can include a free change, a fee, a maximum percentage of the group, a cutoff date, or restrictions close to departure.

Confirm before you promise. Check the carrier's rule (Carrier rules tab), then route the request through the existing thread.

Deviations

A deviation is when a passenger doesn't fly the complete group itinerary.

Core groupLAX → CUN, CUN → LAX
Deviating passengerLAX → CUN with the group, CUN → SFO on return

Depending on the carrier, deviations may be allowed on the return only, on either direction, for a percentage of passengers, with a minimum staying in the core group, with fare differences or a fee, or not at all.

Check the contract first. Always verify the specific group contract before telling the advisor a deviation is possible.

8

Service

From contract to return flight: keep the record clean and the history in one place.

Owned by YouGroup Air

Schedule changes

Customer Air isn't live, so airline schedule changes won't appear in the Classic booking on their own. Group Air emails you the updated itinerary.

  1. Compare the old and new schedule.
  2. Open the Customer Air in Plex.
  3. Select Update.
  4. Change the affected flight number and/or time.
  5. Save the component.
  6. Check the itinerary displays correctly.
  7. Send updated statement/documentation as appropriate.

One email thread, beginning to end

Mandatory. Every message about the group goes on the same Group Air thread: acceptance, deposit questions, payment confirmation, reductions, name lists, name changes, deviations, schedule changes, contract questions, and servicing issues.

Several Flights team members may work the Group Air inbox. One thread means whoever picks it up sees the whole history, and nobody asks the advisor for something twice.

Escalation

Contact Group Air instead of interpreting an airline contract on your own when:

  • Contract language is unclear
  • An unusual servicing issue comes up
  • A request needs more support
  • Something needs escalating

The Flights supervisor overseeing Group Air can step in when further escalation is needed.
